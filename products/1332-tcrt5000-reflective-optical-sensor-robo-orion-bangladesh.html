The TCRT5000 Reflective Optical Sensor is a compact and highly efficient component that combines an infrared (IR) emitter and a phototransistor in a single leaded package. It is the industry standard for hobbyists and engineers building custom line-following arrays or obstacle detection modules. Because this is the bare component (without the PCB), it offers ultimate flexibility for creating custom-shaped sensor bars for robotics.

When the IR LED emits light, it reflects off a nearby surface. If the surface is light-colored (like white paper), the light bounces back to the phototransistor, allowing current to pass. If the surface is dark (like black tape), the light is absorbed, and the phototransistor remains off. The sensor incorporates a daylight blocking filter built into its housing, heavily reducing interference from ambient room lighting.

Perfect for engineering projects, rotary encoders, and mechanical limit switches, the TCRT5000 requires external current-limiting and pull-up resistors to interface with microcontrollers like Arduino and ESP32.

Note: This is the bare 4-pin electronic component. It requires external circuitry (resistors or a comparator IC like the LM393) to function correctly with digital logic systems.

Frequently Asked Questions

The price of the bare TCRT5000 reflective optical sensor component is just BDT 15 at Robo Orion BD, making it incredibly affordable for students building custom line-following arrays.

This product is the bare 4-pin electronic component (comprising the IR LED and phototransistor). It does not include a PCB, comparator chip (like LM393), or onboard resistors. You will need to wire it with appropriate current-limiting and pull-up resistors in your circuit.

The sensor operates effectively within a range of 0.2 mm to 15 mm. However, its peak optical reflection and most accurate readings occur at a distance of approximately 2.5 mm from the reflective surface.

To use this bare sensor with an Arduino, you must connect a current-limiting resistor (e.g., 220 or 330 ohms) in series with the IR LED's anode (+). For the phototransistor, connect its emitter to GND, and its collector to an analog or digital pin, utilizing a 10k ohm pull-up resistor to VCC. As infrared light bounces back, the phototransistor allows current to flow, altering the voltage read by the Arduino.
